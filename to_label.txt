After the window, deletion is irreversible. Profile, authentication, workflows, run logs, and integrations are deleted; audit log entries are anonymized; financial records are retained per tax law.
NOTES: Answer must include 30-day window. Should mention deletion is irreversible after window.
ANSWER: To permanently delete a user and all associated data under GDPR, follow these steps:
1. Navigate to **Settings → Privacy → Data subject requests → New deletion request**.
2. Enter the data subject's email and confirm the request.
3. The deletion will occur within **30 days**. During this 30-day window, the request can be canceled. After the window, deletion is irreversible.

Q038  What's the most efficient way to process a large batch in a workflow?
REFERENCE: Use a Parallel Loop step with concurrency up to 10 if iterations are independent. Sequential loops process one item at a time and can hit the 30-minute workflow timeout. Parallel loops can give up to 10x speedup. Be cautious of downstream rate limits — running 10 calls in parallel can blow through APIs that throttle aggressively.
NOTES: Answer must mention Parallel Loop and the concurrency cap.
ANSWER: The most efficient way to process a large batch in a workflow is to use a **Parallel Loop** step if the iterations are independent. This allows you to process up to 10 items concurrently, significantly reducing the overall runtime compared to sequential processing. For example, if you have 5000 records, using a Parallel Loop can decrease the runtime from approximately 33 minutes (if processed sequentially) to around 3.3 minutes (when processed in parallel). However, be cautious of potential rate limits from any external APIs being called within the loop.

Q046  Customer says they were charged for an overage but they're sure they were under their limit. How do we investigate?

Check usage history in Settings → Billing → Usage to see actual run counts and identify the top contributing workflows. If the customer was indeed over, walk through which workflows drove it — common pattern is duplicate webhook subscriptions causing one workflow to fire many times more than expected. Confirm they received the standard 80%/100%/125% usage alert emails (often filtered to a folder). For first-time overages, a goodwill credit (typically 50%) can be offered after CSM judgment. Document in the customer's record per refund policy.
NOTES: Answer must walk through the diagnosis (verify usage, find root cause workflow) AND the credit option.
ANSWER: To investigate a customer's claim of being charged for an overage while believing they were under their limit, follow these steps:

1. **Check the Customer's Plan**: Verify the customer's subscription plan and its included run limit.

2. **Review Usage Data**: Access the usage data by navigating to **Settings → Billing → Usage**. This will show the current month's run count compared to the plan limit, a daily run breakdown, and the top workflows by run count.

3. **Identify Overage Charges**: If the run count exceeds the plan limit, calculate the overage charges based on the pricing structure for their plan. For example, for Pro plans, overage runs are billed at $0.01 per additional run.

4. **Check for Alerts**: Determine if the customer received any automated usage alerts regarding their run count. This can help identify if they were informed about nearing or exceeding their limit.

5. **Communicate Findings**: Once you have gathered the necessary information, communicate your findings to the customer, explaining the breakdown of their charges and any relevant usage data.
